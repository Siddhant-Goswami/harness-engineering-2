The scorecard

How a brief is scored

+1 for each link that mattered and made the brief

+2 for each decision it called right

−½ for each noise item it called important

Divided by everything there was to find. Links cited under “Who disagrees” are not scored either way.

Week 1, first setup

Found 7 of 9 links that mattered

Both decisions right

Called 3 noise items important

(7 + 4 − 1.5) ÷ 13

0.73

Why the penalty? The cheapest way to find every link that mattered is to cite all of them.

TIME: minute 20 to 22. Short. The scoring is plain code, not a model, and the room should see that it fits on a slide. Walk the three lines, then the worked example on the right. The example is real: it is week one of the sample on the first setup, where every run scored 0.731. ASK before the click: “If you were the agent and wanted a perfect score, what is the laziest thing you could do?” Someone will say “mention everything”. CLICK. That is why noise costs half a point. Citing all 25 links in week one would score about 0.38, well under the 0.73 above. Point at the last grey line: a brief is allowed to quote the weak side of an argument under “Who disagrees” without being punished for it. The three numbers (1, 2 and one half) sit at the top of verifier/score.py. They are placeholders until you have run it on your own month. Tell the room they can change them, as long as they do it BEFORE the agents run and not after seeing a score. Open question to flag honestly: the weights were set on a made-up month.
