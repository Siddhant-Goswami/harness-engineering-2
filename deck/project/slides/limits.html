Before you trust it

What this run does not prove

The month was made up

Kiln, Orchard and Anvil are fictional. The traps were designed to repeat every week, so week four was always going to reward learning the reader’s tools.

Three runs is still small

Averaging shrinks the noise; it does not remove it. Kept rounds are partly the lucky ones.

Decisions were too easy

The first setup got every decision on weeks 1 to 3 right, so every gain came from links.

The weights are placeholders

One point a link, two a decision, half off for noise. Set yours on your own month, before you look at a score.

Your own month is the real test.

TIME: minute 83 to 85. Do not skip this slide, even if you are running late. Overclaiming here would undo the whole session. Say each card in one sentence. The sample month is fiction, and its traps repeat every week by design. That is good for teaching and it flatters the hidden week. A real month will not repeat this neatly. Three runs per week is a small sample. The rise on week four was larger than its noise, but on a harder month a 0.02 gain could easily be luck. Decisions: in the recorded run the first setup got all six decisions on weeks 1 to 3 right, so the scoring of decisions did no work. A real month should include at least one decision where the loud answer was wrong. Weights: the three numbers at the top of verifier/score.py were chosen once and never tuned. Then the wider honesty, from the brief: full self-improvement is not here and is not inevitable. What we built is narrow. It edits the layer on top of a native harness, against your own hindsight, behind a fence, with a human reading the diffs. CLICK: “Your own month is the real test.” That is the take-home.
